3. **Clan Relationships**: Clans were larger kinship units beyond immediate family or direct lineages and often governed by shared ancestry myths that provided a sense of identity and belonging to their members. These systems helped transmit cultural knowledge and expectations from one generation to the next.

Arranged marriages were common, with some societies practicing polygamy based on social status or as a means for building alliances.

6. **Land Tenure Systems**: Land was typically communal property within many African communities, managed by kinship groups and the clan in collaboration with chiefs or other local authority figures. The land rights were linked to lineage membership and often involved complex ceremonies during transfers of control.

10. **Conflict Resolution**: Within these systems, resolving disputes—whether interpersonal, intra-familial, or communal—required understanding kinship connections and consulting with the appropriate lineage elders or councils, demonstrating a blend of tradition and social order.

11. **Colonial Impact**: Colonization disrupted many African societies' ways of life by imposing European systems of land tenure, legal structures, marriage practices, and governance models that often eroded traditional kinship roles and alliances.
