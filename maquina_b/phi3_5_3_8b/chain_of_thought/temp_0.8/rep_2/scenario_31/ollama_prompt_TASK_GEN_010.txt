**Follow-up Question 1: How does Lidar technology contribute uniquely to monitoring urban infrastructure compared to other remote sensing technologies?

**Elaborated Textbook-Level Solution:**  
Lidar, which stands for Light Detection and Ranging, represents a cutting-edge remote sensing technology that provides high-resolution topographic data by utilizing laser pulses. Unlike traditional photogrammetry, Lidar can penetrate through vegetation, allowing it to capture detailed ground surface elevations beneath tree canopies or within urban foliage. This feature is particularly useful for monitoring cities where the natural and built environments often intermix.

When assessing urban infrastructure—roads, buildings, bridges—Lidar's precision enables an accurate 3D representation of these structures by creating detailed point clouds from which Digital Elevation Models (DEM) can be generated. These models serve as foundational elements for understanding the morphology and potential areas in need of repair or upgrades due to wear or age-related deterioration, especially beneficial before undertaking large construction projects where precise measurements are critical.

Lidar technology excels at monitoring urban sprawl by mapping land use changes over time with high accuracy. It can detect minute alterations in the landscape caused by development activities, thereby enabling city planners to analyze growth patterns effectively and make informed decisions for future expansion plans. Furthermore, Lidar's ability to operate day or night under most weather conditions—provided there is sufficient lighting (either natural or from ground-based sources)—allows consistent data collection that isn't limited by solar angles, as some photogrammetric methods would be.

Additionally, the integration of Lidar with Geographic Information Systems (GIS) empowers urban planners and decision makers to visualize complex datasets spatially and perform advanced analyses like viewshed or shadow mapping—tasks that are invaluable when planning city structures for optimal lighting conditions.
