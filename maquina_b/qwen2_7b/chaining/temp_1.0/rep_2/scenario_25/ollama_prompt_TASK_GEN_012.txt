As an Earth-observation mission operator, I am tasked with capturing essential data on Indonesia's ecosystem dynamics. Focusing on the city of Ecuatorial, my request aims to gather insights through a VNIR sensor at night, providing clear images during the day after tomorrow overnight. Recognizing this task as a low priority importance, it's framed as an opportunistic capture with flexible scheduling. This "background survey" serves as a valuable supplement to our baseline pipeline tracking. It enables us to assess chlorophyll absorption levels, calculate the NDVI index, and gather surface reflectance data without imposing strict deadlines or operational urgencies. By utilizing our sensor in this opportune manner, we will gather accurate and useful information for future assessments and decision-making processes.
