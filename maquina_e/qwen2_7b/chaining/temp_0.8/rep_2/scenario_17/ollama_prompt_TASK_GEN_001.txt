Nestled within the lush green landscapes of San Martín, Colombia, a low priority importance task has been initiated to capture the essence of its vibrant vegetation using the near-infrared (NIR) sensor. This opportunity arises amidst the stillness of the night when the task is set to occur "in four days overnight". With a flexible schedule and no rush, this background survey aims to provide valuable insights into water body boundaries and soil moisture assessments without any constraints or urgency implications. The sensor's capability to discriminate gradients in the environment promises detailed mapping capabilities essential for understanding ecological conditions, enabling a deeper knowledge base for environmental management without the pressure of an un-preemptable mission lock or binding contract requirement.
