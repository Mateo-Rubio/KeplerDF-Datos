Intrinsic Motivation encompasses personal enjoyment and the internal fulfillment one gains from activities driven by interests and satisfaction without reliance on external rewards, whereas Extrinsic Motivation is propelled by external factors like rewards or avoiding punishment. Intrinsic motivators tend to lead to long-term engagement in tasks for pleasure or personal growth; extrinsic motives often result in short-term focus and immediate gratification but may not always sustain interest when such external stimuli are removed. The interaction between these forms of motivation suggests a balance can be achieved, where individuals benefit from both internal drivers (autonomy, mastery) and appropriate use of external rewards to maintain performance and continued engagement in their tasks or activities.
