In preparation for a comprehensive environmental survey of AMCO, Area Metropolitana Centro Occidente, Colombia, an opportunity has arisen to capture thermal characteristics of its landscape using Near-Infrared (NIR) sensors. This low priority importance request involves an assessment of water body boundaries and soil moisture levels around mid-day on the day after tomorrow as our deadline constraint. As part of this opportunistic capture task, we aim to gather invaluable data for understanding the region's ecological dynamics without placing any undue stress on the operational timeline.

This background survey is designated for flexible scheduling and will be treated as a non-essential background filler task, allowing for potential rescheduling should more critical operational tasks require priority allocation. With this "testbed tracking task" status, our primary objective remains to contribute to an ongoing understanding of regional environmental conditions without impeding more pressing operational commitments.

The rationale behind this request is rooted in the need to evaluate soil moisture content and delineate water body boundaries for future agricultural planning, ecological studies, and potential resource management. By aligning this observational task with the operational calendar as outlined, we ensure a comprehensive yet opportunistic approach to data collection that supports our broader Earth-observation mission objectives without causing disruptions to existing high-priority missions.
