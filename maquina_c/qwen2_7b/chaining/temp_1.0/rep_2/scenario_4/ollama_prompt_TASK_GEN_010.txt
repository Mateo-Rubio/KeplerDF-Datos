The images are essential for monitoring environmental conditions, land use changes, urban development, and conservation efforts in Prado during daytime hours when the satellite passes overhead on the day after tomorrow.

Leveraging our satellite constellation's capabilities, we will undertake an active radar scan using SAR technology to capture ground geometry beneath cloudy or stormy skies. This microwave surface imaging technique provides a unique perspective on Prado's urban and rural landscapes by penetrating cloud cover, revealing critical topographical details that might not be observable otherwise. This "cloud-penetrating capture" will serve as a valuable addition to our asset management and baseline tracking efforts.

The goal here is to detect heat signatures and establish a surface temperature profile across the city. Our aim is to assess chlorophyll absorption levels and calculate a Vegetation Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI) across various ecosystems within the city. This data will help us understand land use dynamics, agricultural practices, and the overall health of the local environment.

Our efforts aim to ensure that this "background survey" remains a flexible schedule activity that can be dropped or rescheduled as per operational needs.
