In three days, at the dead of night when the stars are out and the world is asleep, our satellite constellation will be tasked with capturing essential data from Colombia's Samacá region. Given the high priority level, this mission allocation is an absolute institutional priority involving a critical payload capture. The operational execution must be urgent, ensuring that this "overnight" deadline is met without any preemption or delay.

The primary sensor to be deployed for this mission will be the TIR (Thermal Infrared) sensor, which will provide thermal mapping data essential for detecting heat signatures and surface temperature profiles across the region. This data collection is vital for thermal anomaly detection and understanding heat emissions in the area.

As part of our baseline pipeline tracking with fixed parameters representing normal operational status, this tasking is part of a standard workflow update. The imagery captured will serve as a crucial background survey and testbed tracking task that can be flexibly scheduled according to operational convenience.

This mission's execution is critical for maintaining a robust observational capability across diverse geographical contexts, ensuring that our satellite constellation remains responsive to the varied demands of Earth observation missions.
