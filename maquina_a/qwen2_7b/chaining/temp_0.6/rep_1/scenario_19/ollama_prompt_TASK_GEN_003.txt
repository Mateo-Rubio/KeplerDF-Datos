In accordance with our binding contract requirement for a critical payload capture, we hereby request an absolute institutional priority mission to gather thermal mapping data of the Ecuatorial region in Indonesia. This task involves observing surface temperature profiles and detecting thermal anomalies during an active radar scan using microwave surface imaging. The goal is to ensure cloud-penetrating capture and gain insights into ground geometry without interference from heavy weather conditions.

As a standard operational tasking with fixed parameters, this request falls under our baseline pipeline tracking setup. With normal operational status and a routine monitoring protocol, we aim for an efficient process that incorporates the necessity of thermal anomaly detection as part of our ongoing workflow update.

The deadline for this task is set to be fulfilled "in three days overnight." This upper limit represents the time frame for data availability, ensuring timely information without compromising accuracy or quality. 

For this mission, we are employing a TIR sensor to capture high-resolution optical snapshots for daylight photography, clear optical imagery, and true-color visual documentation. The sensor's capability to assess chlorophyll absorption levels through VNIR data will provide valuable insights into the vegetation health and NDVI index calculation.

In addition, the NIR sensor will be used for near-infrared reflection analysis to map water body boundaries and assess soil moisture levels, providing critical information on environmental conditions that cannot be obtained through traditional methods. 

All efforts are focused on delivering the highest quality data that meets our operational guidelines for naturalness and validation accuracy. We emphasize the importance of this mission to ensure comprehensive coverage and accurate mapping for our ongoing Earth observation projects.
