In three days during the morning in Uribe, Colombia, an automated satellite constellation operator is tasked with conducting a standard operational SAR scan for cloud-penetrating capture. The mission involves detailed microwave surface imaging to collect information on the ground geometry without interference from heavy weather conditions. As this task carries a low importance level, it is considered a routine monitoring assignment within the baseline pipeline tracking setup, maintaining normal operational status. With fixed parameters set in place, the system will ensure that this task operates under standard workflows while allowing for flexible scheduling to fit into an opportunistic capture scenario. This "background survey" for data collection may optionally be dropped or rescheduled as per the operator's convenience, serving as a testbed tracking task without significant impact on operational priorities.
