When people are driven by external factors such as rewards, recognition, or punishments, their behaviors may not align with their internal values or intentions. This type of motivation can lead to actions that prioritize short-term gains over long-term well-being or ethical considerations.

External motivators are often effective in prompting immediate compliance or performance but may not foster genuine engagement, satisfaction, or the development of intrinsic motivation. Intrinsic motivation, on the other hand, is driven by internal rewards such as personal growth, enjoyment, or a sense of purpose and is more likely to lead to fulfilling and sustained behavior over time.

Here are some examples illustrating extrinsically motivated behaviors:

1. Employees working overtime to receive extra pay beyond their contractual salary. The primary driving force here is the financial incentive rather than a passion for work or career advancement.

2. Students who complete assignments solely for the promise of a good grade, not out of interest in the subject matter itself but because they seek approval from teachers or to avoid punishment like failing grades.

3. Parents rewarding their children with candy for cleaning their room, where the act is driven by the immediate pleasure of receiving treats rather than developing a habit of keeping spaces tidy out of personal responsibility and values.

4. Athletes who train intensely under strict dietary restrictions to win competitions or gain sponsorships, with less focus on enjoying sports for its own sake and more on external accolades.

5. Consumers purchasing luxury goods not because they need them but to display wealth and status among peers, motivated by social recognition rather than personal utility of the products.

In each scenario, extrinsic factors heavily influence behavior, which can sometimes lead to positive outcomes like short-term achievements or adherence to important rules. However, these external rewards may not promote deeper engagement with activities and could potentially undermine self-motivation and intrinsic satisfaction when those external incentives are removed. For long-lasting behavior change and genuine interest, fostering internal motivators is crucial.
