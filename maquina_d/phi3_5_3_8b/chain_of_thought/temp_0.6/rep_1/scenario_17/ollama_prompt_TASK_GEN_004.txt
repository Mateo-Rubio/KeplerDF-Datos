To understand Earth's evolutionary journey from its inception around 4.6 billion years ago, it's essential to delve into key events that shaped our planet: The collapse of a solar nebula formed the Sun, with surrounding planetesimals coalescing into protoplanets; internal heating led Earth to differentiate internally and form distinct layers; cometary impacts contributed volatiles for an early atmosphere rich in water vapor; the giant impact hypothesis posits that our Moon resulted from a colossal collision billions of years ago. Photosynthetic organisms later evolved, leading to significant atmospheric changes through oxygen production—a pivotal moment often referred to as the Great Oxygenation Event around 2.5 billion years ago. Earth's climate and weather systems began taking shape with wind, clouds, and precipitation driving erosion and sedimentation processes; plate tectonics further sculpted our planet starting about 2.5 billion years ago through continental drift. Finally, the appearance of complex multicellular organisms roughly 600 million years ago marked a new era in life's complexity on Earth, all underpinned by an atmosphere that was conducive to such biodiversity—a testament to the intricate and interconnected nature of planetary evolution.
