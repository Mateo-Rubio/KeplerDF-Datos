In preparation for tomorrow's fMRI study focusing on emotion recognition from faces, please arrange a session that will analyze the neural correlates of processing emotional stimuli during face perception. Specifically, observe and document the involvement of key brain regions like the amygdala in detecting basic emotions such as fear or anger, while also considering higher-order cognitive engagement wherein structures such as the prefrontal cortex contribute to interpreting complex social cues for appropriate behavior modification. Special attention should be given to the interplay between these regions during face recognition tasks—pay close scrutiny on how both ventral and dorsal visual processing streams come together, with additional insights from areas like the anterior cingulate cortex (ACC), orbitofrontal cortex (OFC), and their role in assessment of rewards or punishments related to emotional expressions. We are particularly interested in understanding how these neural processes unfold over time as the subject is exposed to a series of facial stimuli, ranging from neutral to various complex emotional states.
