This theory proposes that individuals seek to minimize the negative effects on their relationship, and as a result, they are more likely to engage in constructive communication aimed at resolving conflicts without resorting to harmful or aggressive actions.

Key Points:
- Concern for relationships influences behavior during conflict resolution; efforts are made to maintain positive connections with others.
- Constructive communication becomes a priority when the motivation is preserving interpersonal bonds rather than winning an argument.
- The desire to mitigate negative relational impact serves as a guidepost for how individuals approach and navigate through conflicts.

Contrast:
In contrast, conflict resolution driven by extrinsic motivators focuses on external rewards or avoidance of punishment. Here, the behavior is more self-centered, with actions taken primarily to benefit oneself rather than fostering harmony among parties involved in a dispute. Strategies employed may involve negotiation for personal gain, manipulating others' perceptions to one’s favor, or withdrawing from communication altogether when it could lead to negative consequences, regardless of the impact on relationships.

Example Scenario:
Consider two coworkers who have opposing views during a meeting about company restructuring. The intrinsically motivated individual approaches resolution by voicing concerns respectfully and seeking compromise that benefits all involved while maintaining their working relationship with peers, valuing camaraderie as much as the disagreement itself. They recognize that a positive rapport has broader implications beyond just resolving current differences, understanding cooperation as essential for continued team success.

Extrinsically motivated behaviors might manifest when one coworker seeks to assert dominance in restructuring debates, aiming primarily at personal advancement or power dynamics rather than the collective good of the company. This individual may resort to intimidation tactics, blame games, or threats and could possibly retract from open communication with colleagues if perceived actions don't secure a desired outcome for themselves alone.

In these scenarios:
- Intrinsically motivated individuals engage in dialogue aimed at resolving the conflict while preserving relationships through empathetic listening, understanding, and shared compromise.
- Extrinsic motivators are characterized by competitive negotiation tactics, focusing on personal benefit with minimal regard for maintaining a collaborative atmosphere among colleagues.

Real-life Applications:
Understanding the dynamics of intrinsic versus extrinsic conflict resolution can be essential in various fields such as education (where student behavior management), workplace relations (negotiating mergers or resolving disputes between employees and management), healthcare (teamwork amongst medical staff with differing opinions on patient care), and social settings (mediating personal disagreements within families).
