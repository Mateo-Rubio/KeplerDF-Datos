Intrinsic motivation refers to doing something because it is inherently interesting or enjoyable, while extrinsic motivation involves performing an activity to earn a reward or avoid punishment outside of the activity itself. A more intrinsically motivating approach might involve engaging with tasks that satisfy internal needs and provide personal satisfaction, such as pursuing activities for their own sake or aligning closely with one's values and interests.

Intrinsic Motivation Example:
A student reads a challenging novel solely because they enjoy unraveling complex plots and characters without any external rewards in mind (like grades or accolades). They read for the sheer pleasure of escaping into another world, satisfying their love for literature.

Extrinsic Motivation Example:
The same student reads to earn points that contribute towards a prize at school—a reward they can get only if they perform well in an essay about what they've read. Their focus is on the external reward rather than the enjoyment of reading itself.

Improving Intrinsic Motivation:
To foster intrinsic motivation, one could create learning environments that encourage curiosity and self-directed exploration—like educational programs where students can freely pursue topics they are passionate about or have inherent questions about. When a person feels autonomy (having control over their choices), competence (feeling skilled at tasks they're engaged in), relatedness (felt belonging and connection with others around the task), intrinsic motivation is more likely to thrive, according to Self-Determination Theory (Deci & Ryan, 1985).

Here are some strategies that can improve an individual’s intrinsic motivation:

1. Offer Autonomy: Allow students or workers the freedom to set their own goals and manage how they work towards them. This could mean letting children pick which books they want to read from a selection, rather than dictating every title. Employees can be given flexible scheduling or remote work options when possible.

2. Focus on Mastery: Emphasize skill development over rote task completion or scoring high marks. If students understand that the reading isn't just about finishing novels but also understanding deeper themes and vocabulary, they may read out of genuine interest rather than compulsion to meet standards.

3. Make Learning Purposeful: Connect tasks with personal relevance; show how individual efforts contribute meaningfully beyond immediate circumstances—like reading leading to improved communication skills or empathy.

4. Provide Meaning and Significance: Communicate the importance of each task, relating it back to broader life goals or societal impacts when appropriate, so that tasks are not seen as disconnected fragments but integral pieces in a larger puzzle (this might involve writing about how literature helps shape cultural understanding).

5. Encourage Curiosity: Stimulate curiosity with thought-provoking questions and discussions—prompting students or colleagues to think deeper into subjects beyond their current knowledge base can spur an internal desire for discovery.

6. Offer Feedback That Values Process Over Product: Praise effort, strategies used, and the learning journey rather than just outcomes or end products, fostering a mindset where intrinsic values are recognized—like saying "I notice how you're trying different approaches to understand this chapter," instead of just grading papers.

7. Model Intrinsic Interest: Teachers and parents can share their own genuine passion for subjects like reading or science, making it infectious by example. When individuals witness the intrinsic joy in others’ efforts, they may be inspired to experience similar feelings themselves.

To transition a person from extrinsic to more intrinsically motivated behavior, gradually removing external rewards and shifting towards these methods requires patience and consistency. It's also essential to remember that individuals might have various reasons for their current level of engagement; some may require significant time or different conditions before internal satisfaction is reached.

Here are more detailed considerations:

- Self-referent Goal Setting: When setting personal objectives, ask people what they find personally rewarding and incorporate those into daily routines—if a student enjoys space exploration, perhaps reading science fiction aligns with their interests.
  
- Environment Enrichment: Create an atmosphere that promotes intrinsic motivation, for example, by providing quiet spaces for deep study or collaborative discussions where students feel connected and engaged in shared learning.

- Encouraging Reflective Thinking: After tasks, ask individuals to reflect on what they enjoyed about the process—didn't know much about astrophysics? Discuss how reading helped with that interest, reinforcing positive internal feedback loops.
  
- Personal Growth Orientation: Emphasize the benefits of learning for personal development; rather than grades being a primary focus, underscore how knowledge contributes to understanding oneself and one's world better—how might studying literature improve empathy or moral reasoning?

Intrinsic motivation can lead to more sustainable engagement as it aligns with individuals’ core values.
